# Sprint 5: Shipping, presenting and reflecting

Get a project live safely, present it clearly and turn the experience into evidence of your skills.

## Lesson 5.1: Releasing safely

Shipping is a skill, not a moment. The aim is to make releases boring.

- Release small and often. A small change is easy to understand and easy to undo.
- Keep secrets in environment variables or a secrets manager, never in the repository.
- Know how to roll back before you deploy, not after something breaks.
- After a release, look at it: open the site, try the main journey, read the logs.
- Never test with real people's data.

If a release goes wrong, fix the situation first, then write down what happened and what will prevent it, without blaming a person.

---

## Lesson 5.2: Demos and storytelling

A good demo is a short story about a person and their problem, not a tour of features.

- Open with who the product is for and the problem they have.
- Show one real journey from start to finish. Rehearse it so it works the first time.
- Say what you chose not to build and why.
- Be honest about what is unfinished and what you would do next.
- Let every team member present a part.

Keep it under ten minutes and leave time for questions.

---

## Lesson 5.3: Turning the programme into evidence

Employers trust evidence more than claims. Collect it as you go, not at the end.

- Keep your pull requests and the work you can link to.
- Write one line per project in the form: what I did, how, and what changed because of it.
- Ask your teammates and mentors for specific feedback while it is fresh.
- Put your best work on your GitHub or portfolio, with a README that explains it.

Your certificate can be verified online, so it is worth linking from your profile.
