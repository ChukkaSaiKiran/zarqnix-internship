# Sprint 2: Understanding the problem and designing it

Turn a vague idea into user stories, a backlog and a simple design the team agrees on.

## Lesson 2.1: User stories and acceptance criteria

A user story describes a need from the person's point of view, using one sentence.

```
As a student, I want to reset my password, so that I can get back in if I forget it.
```

A story is not finished until it can be checked. Acceptance criteria are the checks, written so anyone can answer yes or no.

- Given I am on the sign-in page, when I choose reset, then I am asked for my email.
- Given I submit a valid email, then I am told a link was sent, whether or not the account exists.

Keep each story small enough to finish within a sprint. If it cannot, split it by the steps a person takes, not by the layers of the code.

---

## Lesson 2.2: Building and ordering a backlog

The backlog is the ordered list of everything the team might build. The Product Owner owns the order, and the order should follow value to the user and risk to the project.

- Do the riskiest unknowns early, while changing course is cheap.
- Slice thinly: a thin working path from start to finish beats a half-built wide feature.
- Say what is out of scope. Writing down what you will not do protects the sprint.

Use your sprint board's Backlog column for this. Keep titles short and start them with a verb.

---

## Lesson 2.3: Design basics: wireframes and usability

A wireframe is a rough, colourless sketch of a screen. It is deliberately plain so people argue about layout and flow rather than colours.

### Usability checks that catch most problems

- One main action per screen, visually obvious.
- Labels say what will happen: 'Save draft', not 'Submit'.
- Show errors next to the field, say what to fix, and keep what the person already typed.
- Text contrast and size must be readable. Do not rely on colour alone to carry meaning.
- Every control must work with a keyboard.

Test with a real person early. Five minutes watching someone use a sketch beats an hour of opinions.
